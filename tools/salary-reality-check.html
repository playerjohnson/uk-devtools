Is Your Salary Enough for Your City?

Knowing your gross salary doesn't tell you much on its own. What matters is what's left after the taxman takes their share and after you've covered the basics — rent, bills, food, and getting to work. This calculator bridges that gap by combining a full UK tax calculation with real city-specific living costs, giving you a single honest number: your monthly surplus or deficit.

Living costs vary enormously across the UK. A £30,000 salary that leaves you comfortable in Leeds could leave you struggling in London, where rents are roughly double. Council tax, energy bills, and transport costs all shift depending on where you live. This tool accounts for those differences using current 2025/26 data so you can compare cities and make informed decisions about job offers, relocations, or salary negotiations.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is take-home pay calculated?
We deduct Income Tax (using 2025/26 bands for England/Wales/NI or Scotland), employee National Insurance (8% on £12,570–£50,270, 2% above), your pension contribution, and any student loan repayments. The result is your monthly net pay — the amount that actually hits your bank account.
Where do the living cost figures come from?
Our city cost profiles are based on the latest available data from ONS, Ofgem (energy price cap at £1,758/year for a medium household), regional council tax band D averages, and rental market data from sources like Rightmove and ONS private rental indices. All figures represent typical costs for a single adult and are updated regularly.
Can I adjust the living costs?
Yes. Click "Customise living costs" to override any category with your actual figure. If you know your exact rent, energy bill, or council tax, enter it for a more accurate result. Any field left blank uses the city average.
What do the lifestyle tiers mean?
Surviving means your costs meet or exceed your income — no room for savings or unexpected expenses. Tight means less than 10% left over, which leaves little financial breathing room. Stable is 10–20% surplus, enough for modest savings. Comfortable is 20–35% surplus, giving you room for holidays, savings goals, and lifestyle spending. Thriving means 35%+ surplus — strong financial freedom.
Does this include all living costs?
We cover the main recurring costs: housing, council tax, energy, water, broadband, groceries, transport, and phone. We don't include discretionary spending (eating out, entertainment, clothing, subscriptions) or one-off costs (holidays, car repairs, insurance). Your actual surplus will be lower once discretionary spending is factored in.
Is £30,000 a good salary in the UK?
It depends entirely on where you live and your housing situation. On £30,000, your take-home is roughly £1,990 per month. In Leeds or Glasgow, that could leave you with a comfortable surplus after essentials. In London, you'd likely struggle to cover rent alone without a flat share. Use this calculator with your specific city and housing situation to find out.
How does Scottish Income Tax differ?
Scotland has six tax bands instead of three: Starter (19%), Basic (20%), Intermediate (21%), Higher (42%), Advanced (45%), and Top (48%). This means Scottish taxpayers earning above £43,663 pay more income tax than equivalent earners in England. National Insurance is the same across the UK. Select "Scotland" as your tax region for accurate Scottish calculations.
